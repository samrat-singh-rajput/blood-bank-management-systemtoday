# Donor Eligibility Guidelines

**Source:** Blood Bank Management System Educational Reference  
**Category:** Donor Criteria & Health Protocols  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Last Updated:** September 2026  

---

## 1. Core Qualifications for Donating Blood

To donate whole blood, donors must meet general health requirements established to protect both the donor's well-being and the recipient's safety:

- **Age Requirement:** Donors must be between 18 and 65 years of age. First-time donors over the age of 60 may require approval from a medical officer.
- **Body Weight:** Donors must weigh at least 50 kg (110 lbs) for a standard 350 ml or 450 ml whole blood collection.
- **Vital Signs:**
  - Blood Pressure: Systolic between 100–140 mmHg and Diastolic between 60–90 mmHg.
  - Pulse / Heart Rate: Regular, between 60 and 100 beats per minute.
  - Body Temperature: Oral temperature must be normal (36.5°C to 37.5°C or 97.7°F to 99.5°F).
- **Hemoglobin Levels:** Minimum 12.5 g/dL for both male and female donors. A finger-prick screening test confirms this prior to collection.

---

## 2. Donation Intervals and Frequency

The human body needs time to replenish its red blood cells and iron reserves between donation sessions:

- **Whole Blood Donation:** Minimum waiting interval is 56 days (8 weeks) for male donors and 90 days (12 weeks) for female donors.
- **Platelet Donation (Apheresis):** Can be performed every 7 to 14 days, up to a maximum of 24 times per year.
- **Plasma Donation:** Can be donated every 28 days.

---

## 3. Temporary Deferral Conditions

Temporary deferrals mean an individual cannot donate today, but will be welcome to donate once the deferral period expires:

- **Minor Illness or Infection:** Active cold, cough, sore throat, or fever requires deferral until fully symptom-free for at least 7 days.
- **Antibiotic Therapy:** Must wait at least 7 to 14 days after completing the last dose of antibiotics.
- **Dental Procedures:** Minor cleaning or filling requires a 24-hour wait; major dental surgery requires a 72-hour to 1-month wait depending on healing.
- **Tattoos and Body Piercings:** A 6-month deferral is standard if performed at an unregulated or non-certified facility to prevent bloodborne pathogen risks.
- **Travel to Malaria-Endemic Zones:** A 3 to 12-month deferral is required depending on travel destination, prophylaxis, and symptoms.
- **Pregnancy and Breastfeeding:** Women must wait 6 months post-childbirth and must have concluded primary breastfeeding before donating.
- **Recent Vaccinations:** Most inactivated vaccines (COVID-19 mRNA, Influenza) allow immediate donation if asymptomatic. Live attenuated vaccines (MMR, Yellow Fever) require a 4-week waiting period.

---

## 4. Permanent Deferral Conditions

Individuals with certain chronic or high-risk medical conditions are permanently excluded from blood donation for their own health or recipient safety:

- Positive tests or high risk for bloodborne viral infections: Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), Hepatitis B Virus (HBV), Hepatitis C Virus (HCV).
- Chronic cardiovascular conditions, including congestive heart failure, severe arrhythmias, or history of myocardial infarction.
- Chronic renal failure or severe active liver disease.
- Hematological malignancies, leukemia, lymphoma, or chronic bleeding disorders (such as severe hemophilia).
- History of organ, bone marrow, or cornea transplants from bovine or high-risk human sources.

---

## 5. Donor Preparation Checklist

To ensure a smooth donation experience and prevent vasovagal reactions (lightheadedness or fainting):

1. **Hydration:** Drink at least 500 ml (2 large glasses) of water or fruit juice 30 to 60 minutes before arriving.
2. **Nutrition:** Eat a balanced, iron-rich meal within 2 to 3 hours prior to donation. Avoid high-fat meals (such as fried foods) as fats interfere with blood testing.
3. **Rest:** Ensure a restful night of sleep (at least 7 hours) prior to donation day.
4. **Clothing:** Wear comfortable clothing with loose sleeves that can be rolled up past the elbow easily.
5. **Identification:** Bring valid government-issued photo identification and your Blood Bank registered credentials.
