## 1. General Donation Inquiries

### Does donating blood hurt?
You will feel a brief stinging or pinching sensation for just one second when the needle is inserted. After that, the collection itself is completely painless. The staff are trained professionals dedicated to your comfort.

### How much blood is taken, and how long does it take for my body to replenish it?
A standard donation collects approximately 350 ml to 450 ml of whole blood, which is less than 10% of an adult's average blood volume (around 5 liters). Your body replaces the liquid plasma volume within 24 to 48 hours, provided you drink adequate fluids. Red blood cells take approximately 4 to 6 weeks to be completely regenerated by the bone marrow, which is why waiting periods between donations exist.

### Can I contract any diseases or infections by donating blood?
Absolutely not. Every item used during the donation process—including needles, gauze, tubing, and collection bags—is 100% sterile, single-use, and manufactured for one patient only. Equipment is opened directly in front of you and safely incinerated in biohazard containers immediately after use. It is physically impossible to contract HIV, Hepatitis, or any infection by donating blood.

## 2. Eligibility & Lifestyle Questions

### Can vegetarians or vegans donate blood?
Yes, vegetarians and vegans can donate blood without any restrictions, provided their hemoglobin levels meet the minimum requirement of 12.5 g/dL. Plant-based donors can maintain adequate iron by consuming foods such as spinach, lentils, chickpeas, fortified cereals, beans, pumpkin seeds, and pairing them with Vitamin C (oranges, tomatoes) to enhance non-heme iron absorption.

### Can people with high blood pressure or taking hypertension medications donate?
Yes, as long as your blood pressure is well-managed and within acceptable limits (systolic 100–140 mmHg and diastolic 60–90 mmHg) at the time of your screening. Most common antihypertensive medications (such as ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and beta-blockers) are acceptable.

### Can people with diabetes donate blood?
Individuals with diabetes can donate if their condition is well-managed through diet or oral medications, and they have not had diabetic complications (such as neuropathy, nephropathy, or vascular disease). In many jurisdictions, individuals who use insulin derived from bovine sources may have specific restrictions; consult the blood bank medical staff for current evaluation.

### Can women donate blood during their menstrual cycle?
Yes. Menstruation does not disqualify a woman from donating blood, provided she feels well on the day of donation and meets the 12.5 g/dL hemoglobin minimum threshold.

## 3. Emergency Requests & System Operations

### How do emergency blood requests work in the system?
Patients or hospital coordinators can submit an urgent requisition via the User Panel. The request specifies the required blood group, units needed, recipient name, hospital name, contact number, and urgency classification (Low, Medium, Critical). Critical requests are highlighted with priority badges and automatically notify matching donors and administrators in the area.

### What are Emergency Access Keys?
Emergency Keys are priority bypass credentials (issued as 'Gold' or 'Platinum' tokens by system administrators). When a hospital or authorized critical patient inputs an Emergency Key during requisition, their request bypasses the standard verification waitlist for immediate allocation from central stocks.

### How is blood tested for safety before it is transfused to a patient?
Every collected unit of whole blood undergoes rigorous mandatory laboratory testing:
1. **ABO & Rh Group Confirmation:** Forward and reverse grouping to verify blood type accurately.
2. **Infectious Disease Screening:** Mandatory screening for Transfusion-Transmissible Infections (TTIs):
   - Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV-1 and HIV-2 antibodies/antigens)
   - Hepatitis B Virus (HBsAg surface antigen)
   - Hepatitis C Virus (anti-HCV antibodies)
   - Syphilis (*Treponema pallidum* serology)
   - Malaria parasites
3. **Cross-Matching:** Before transfusion to a specific patient, cross-match testing ensures the recipient's serum does not react against donor red blood cells.
