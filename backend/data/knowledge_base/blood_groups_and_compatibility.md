**Last Updated:** September 2026

## 1. The ABO Blood Group System

Blood types are determined by the presence or absence of specific inherited protein and sugar markers (antigens) on the surface of red blood cells, as well as naturally occurring antibodies circulating in the blood plasma:

- **Group A:** Red blood cells possess the A antigen; plasma carries anti-B antibodies.
- **Group B:** Red blood cells possess the B antigen; plasma carries anti-A antibodies.
- **Group AB:** Red blood cells express both A and B antigens; plasma carries neither anti-A nor anti-B antibodies.
- **Group O:** Red blood cells express neither A nor B antigens; plasma contains both anti-A and anti-B antibodies.

If a patient receives an incompatible blood group, antibodies in the recipient's blood attack and lyse the transfused red blood cells, causing an acute hemolytic transfusion reaction, which is a life-threatening medical emergency.

---

## 2. The Rhesus (Rh) Factor

In addition to ABO antigens, red blood cells may carry the Rh surface protein (specifically the D antigen):

- **Rh-Positive (Rh+):** Red blood cells express the Rh D antigen (approximately 85% of the population).
- **Rh-Negative (Rh-):** Red blood cells lack the Rh D antigen (approximately 15% of the population).

Individuals who are Rh-negative will produce anti-Rh antibodies if exposed to Rh-positive red blood cells through an incompatible transfusion or fetomaternal hemorrhage during pregnancy.

## 3. Red Blood Cell Transfusion Compatibility Matrix

For whole blood and packed red blood cells (PRBCs), compatibility is determined by donor antigens versus recipient antibodies:

| Recipient Blood Type | Compatible Donor Red Blood Cells (Can Receive From) | Incompatible Donor Groups |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **O Negative (O-)** | O- only | O+, A+, A-, B+, B-, AB+, AB- |
| **O Positive (O+)** | O+, O- | A+, A-, B+, B-, AB+, AB- |
| **A Negative (A-)** | A-, O- | A+, B+, B-, AB+, AB-, O+ |
| **A Positive (A+)** | A+, A-, O+, O- | B+, B-, AB+, AB- |
| **B Negative (B-)** | B-, O- | B+, A+, A-, AB+, AB-, O+ |
| **B Positive (B+)** | B+, B-, O+, O- | A+, A-, AB+, AB- |
| **AB Negative (AB-)**| AB-, A-, B-, O- | AB+, A+, B+, O+ |
| **AB Positive (AB+)**| AB+, AB-, A+, A-, B+, B-, O+, O- (All Groups) | None (Universal Recipient) |

## 4. Universal Donors and Universal Recipients

- **Universal Red Cell Donor (O Negative):** Because O- red blood cells lack A, B, and Rh D antigens, they will not trigger an immediate ABO or Rh antibody attack in any recipient. O- blood is the frontline resource used during trauma resuscitations when cross-matching is impossible.
- **Universal Red Cell Recipient (AB Positive):** Because AB+ individuals produce neither anti-A, anti-B, nor anti-Rh antibodies, they can safely receive red blood cells from any ABO/Rh group.
- **Universal Plasma Donor (AB):** Plasma contains antibodies rather than red cells. AB plasma lacks anti-A and anti-B antibodies, making AB plasma safe for transfusion into any recipient regardless of their ABO blood type.
- **Universal Plasma Recipient (O):** O individuals can safely receive plasma from any blood group.

## 5. Blood Components and Storage Lifespans

Donated whole blood is rarely transfused intact; it is typically separated into specialized therapeutic components:

1. **Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBC):**
   - Clinical Use: Anemia, severe trauma, surgical hemorrhage.
   - Storage Temperature: 2°C to 6°C in specialized medical refrigeration.
   - Usable Shelf Life: Up to 35 or 42 days (depending on anticoagulant preservative, CPDA-1 or SAGM).
2. **Platelets (Random or Single Donor Apheresis):**
   - Clinical Use: Thrombocytopenia, chemotherapy patients, massive bleeding.
   - Storage Temperature: 20°C to 24°C under constant gentle agitation.
   - Usable Shelf Life: 5 days only due to bacterial contamination risk.
3. **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP):**
   - Clinical Use: Coagulation factor deficiencies, severe liver disease, massive transfusion protocols.
   - Storage Temperature: Frozen at -18°C or colder (ideally -30°C).
   - Usable Shelf Life: Up to 1 year when properly frozen.
4. **Cryoprecipitate:**
   - Clinical Use: Hemophilia A, von Willebrand disease, hypofibrinogenemia.
   - Storage Temperature: Frozen at -18°C or colder.
   - Usable Shelf Life: Up to 1 year.
